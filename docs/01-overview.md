# 1. Overview: Reliability First

[← Repository home](../README.md) · [Next: Reliability Imperative →](02-reliability-imperative.md)

## Core question

**What changes when HRI leaves the laboratory and enters a high-stakes industrial environment?**

The RAISE-HRI perspective is that deployment changes the objective function. In a laboratory, a new interaction method may be evaluated primarily for task performance, intuitiveness, novelty, or user preference. In industry, the same system must also survive degraded sensing, operator turnover, maintenance constraints, safety procedures, throughput pressure, and the consequences of failure.

## Central claim

The repository follows the white paper's main position:

> In high-stakes industrial HRI, interaction innovation is deployable only when it can coexist with operational reliability, understandable failure recovery, multi-role workflows, and calibrated trust.

## Recurrent deployment patterns

The paper identifies four recurring patterns:

- **Reliability imperative** — predictable operation and recoverability constrain how much autonomy or interaction novelty is acceptable.
- **Multi-operator challenge** — industrial systems are commonly used, maintained, inspected, and approved by different people with different information needs.
- **Role-dependent trust** — the operator, maintenance engineer, manager, customer, and regulator may rationally trust the same robot differently.
- **Aspirational–actual user gap** — a system marketed as easy to operate may still require specialist knowledge for configuration and recovery.

These patterns motivate the [four transferable lessons](05-four-transferable-lessons.md).

## What RAISE-HRI is—and is not

RAISE-HRI is a **diagnostic and design resource**, not a new safety standard and not a replacement for risk assessment. It is intended to help teams ask the right socio-technical questions before deployment and during post-deployment review.
