# 6. High-Stakes Industrial HRI Environments

[← Four Transferable Lessons](05-four-transferable-lessons.md) · [Deployment Checklist →](../practitioner/deployment-checklist.md)

## What “high-stakes” means here

RAISE-HRI uses *high-stakes* for environments where robot or interaction failure can create material, safety, regulatory, quality, financial, or operational consequences that make trial-and-error deployment unacceptable.

Examples include:

- inspection and maintenance;
- precision manufacturing;
- collaborative assembly;
- hazardous or difficult-to-access operations;
- safety-sensitive material handling;
- critical infrastructure and mission-oriented robotic work.

## A useful classification

| Consequence dimension | Example question |
|---|---|
| Safety | Could a failure expose a person to harm? |
| Material/asset | Could failure damage equipment, product, or infrastructure? |
| Quality | Could incorrect robot behavior create hidden defects? |
| Regulatory | Must decisions/actions be auditable or compliant? |
| Operational | Does downtime interrupt a production or service chain? |
| Financial | Is a failed cycle or intervention unusually costly? |
| Reputation/trust | Could a visible failure reduce willingness to deploy the system? |

## Deployment mindset

High-stakes does not mean “never innovate.” It means that innovation should be paired with:

- explicit fallback behavior;
- measurable reliability targets;
- role-aware recovery procedures;
- evidence collected under realistic operating conditions;
- clear ownership when the system becomes uncertain.
